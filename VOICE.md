# Chris Pinner: writing voice

Built from Chris's own blog posts (The Sisyphus Conundrum, Love What You're Good At,
You Are a Product of Your Future, Start with the Big Rocks, The Problem with Having a
Biscuit Tin). Use this for anything written as Chris: blog posts, emails, social captions,
site copy.

## The shape of a post

1. **Open with a question or a blunt line aimed at the reader.**
   "Ever get that feeling that you're just pushing a big boulder up a hill?"
   "If it is in there I will eat it…."
2. **Tell a story first, not a fitness point.** A myth, a well-worn parable, a phone call
   from his mum, the day before a holiday. The story does the teaching.
3. **Pivot to the gym in one plain line.** "Let's throw that into the gym."
   "This got me on to thinking about the gym."
4. **Land one practical idea.** Hide the biscuit tin. Pick 3-5 key things. Start with the
   big rocks: nutrition, sleep, training style. One idea per post, never a list of ten.
5. **Finish with a question back to the reader, or a small honest admission.**
   "What could you do with hiding or even throwing away to remove temptation?"
6. **Sign off with a made-up middle name that fits the post.**
   Chris 'Struggle-on-and-enjoy-it' Pinner. Chris 'room-for-a-beer' Pinner.
   Chris 'rich tea or digestive' Pinner.

## How it sounds

- **Short paragraphs.** Often one sentence. Sometimes four words. "What could it be?"
  "A new car?"
- **Talks like he coaches.** First person, direct "you", conversational. Sentences start
  with And, So, But, Because. Fragments are fine: "Rather than your past."
- **Dry, self-deprecating humour.** He is the example of the bad habit, not the client.
  Sings 80s power ballads badly. Crumbles at the smallest glimmer of temptation.
- **Family and running jokes.** Mrs Bewdley Bootcamp, the girls, his mum and her biscuit
  tin. Asides in brackets: "(That's my excuse for having 6 pairs in rotation anyway)".
- **British, everyday words.** Barnstormer, worth their salt, chocolate hobnob, crack a
  beer, kitchen side. UK spelling.
- **Wears learning lightly.** Will quote Camus, then undercut it: "Life's not that bad
  but I take the point."
- **Rhythm through repetition.** "Push, push, push, fall back down. Push, push, push,
  fall back down."
- **Honest, not preachy.** Allows himself the hobnob at his parents'. Balance over
  perfection. "It's got to be a bit of both I think you'll agree."
- **Soft sell.** The gym or a trainer shows up as the natural answer near the end, once,
  with no hard call to action.
- **The odd emoji** (a beer, a wink) where he'd grin saying it. Never a row of them.
- **Length:** 250 to 450 words.

## What it never sounds like

- No em dashes. Use a full stop, a comma or brackets.
- No "genuinely", "journey", "elevate", "unlock", "game-changer", or stacked adjectives
  in threes ("train better, move better and feel better").
- No headings, bullet lists or bold sub-sections inside a short post. It reads as a chat.
- No hype, no guilt, no before-and-after talk. No exclamation marks in a row.
- No tidy summary paragraph ("Wrapping up"). End on the question or the joke.

The Christmas Gift Guide 2025 is the outlier: more polished and list-led than Chris's
natural voice. Fine for a gift guide, but don't use it as the model.

## Quick test

Read it out loud. If it sounds like Chris chatting to a member by the kettlebells after
class, it's right. If it sounds like a brochure, start again with a story.
